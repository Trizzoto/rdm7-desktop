# Captions

One per post, written to be pasted as-is. The voice is the app's own: plain,
specific, no adjectives doing work a number could do. Hashtags are at the
bottom — Instagram treats the first line as the hook, so nothing important goes
below the fold.

**Suggested run:** 01 → 07 → 02 → 10 → 05 → 12 → 03 → 08 → 13 → 04 → 09 → 11 →
06 → 14 → 15 → 16. It alternates lap timing and drift, and holds 16 back as the
closer. 01, 07 and 12 are the three strongest openers if you want a carousel
instead: 01 as the cover, 02–06 inside.

### 01 · The lap
> 1:08.215.
>
> Every metre of it, coloured by how fast you were going. Dark red is slow, pale
> is flat out — so a lap you can only feel becomes a lap you can look at.
>
> Donington National, Lotus Evora GTE, best of seven. 3,147 m at 10 Hz.

### 02 · The delta
> Which lap was faster is the easy question.
>
> Four hundred metres in, this lap was *behind*. It took the whole gap back in
> one corner, held it flat through the middle of the circuit, and found the rest
> on the way home. Final margin: 0.278 s.
>
> Above the line you are losing. Below it you are gaining. Matched by place, not
> by clock.

### 03 · Where the time went
> Seven corners, ranked by what each one cost you.
>
> Nobody placed them — the app finds corners in the lap itself, from speed and
> heading. Turn 1 alone was worth 0.107 s. Every other corner on the lap put
> together came to less than that.
>
> That is the difference between knowing you were slow and knowing where.

### 04 · The ideal lap
> 1:08.126 — a lap you have already driven, just not all at once.
>
> Your best sector 1, your best sector 2, your best sector 3, added up. Not a
> simulation: every piece of it is a piece of a lap you actually drove.
>
> 0.089 s under your best, and all of it already in the data.

### 05 · The grip circle
> One dot per sample, placed by what the tyres were doing.
>
> How hard you were turning, against how hard you were braking or driving. A
> full circle means the tyre was working everywhere.
>
> The empty corners are the free time.
>
> Peak: 1.84 g combined.

### 06 · Moments
> The bits worth watching again, found for you.
>
> Top speed. Hardest braking. Highest cornering. Biggest slide. Best launch.
> Near-spin. Fastest lap.
>
> Click one and the playhead jumps there — and the video jumps with it, because
> they are the same instant.

### 07 · Out of five
> Drift mode scores one corner, on one lap.
>
> Not a session, not a run — the corner. That is the thing you can go and do
> better next time round.
>
> Four parts, all shown beside the stars so the number is never the only thing
> on screen. Angle: 40° held is full marks. Commitment: how much of the corner
> you were actually sideways. Steadiness: from the sideslip rate, so a drift
> held still scores, and one caught and re-caught does not. Speed: against your
> own best at that corner, because what is fast through a corner depends on the
> corner.

### 08 · How sideways
> 48° at the peak. 36.3° held. ±2.9° on the reading.
>
> That last number is the point. The shaded band is the app's own uncertainty,
> drawn — and when it cannot tell you accurately it says so instead of drawing a
> confident line.
>
> The sensor is corrected before you ever see this: this one read 1.8% long and
> sat 0.52°/s off zero, found from 274 places in the drive where the car was
> demonstrably straight.

### 09 · The board
> A good drifter does not drive a complex as three corners.
>
> So the app does not score it as three. Corners you link get read as one unit,
> and the flick between them counts as holding it — not as losing it and
> catching it again.
>
> Turn 7 on this lap is not rated, and it does not score nought. The car went
> round it on the grip. That is not a bad drift, it is not a drift, and nought
> would read as an insult rather than a fact.

### 10 · When not to believe it
> Three times in this session the fix dropped out.
>
> A lap timed across a hole in the recording reads seconds fast, and most tools
> will hand you that lap with a straight face. This one finds every gap, marks
> it, and tells you how far the car travelled while it was not looking: 124 m,
> 226 m, 36 m.
>
> Same for the sensors. The yaw sensor here sat 0.41°/s off zero and read 2.6%
> short. Corrected — and the correction is printed, not hidden inside the
> number.
>
> A tool that never admits doubt is not confident. It is guessing.

### 11 · A car's width
> Two laps a car's width apart look identical on a map.
>
> With the whole circuit on screen a metre is less than one pixel — no stroke is
> thin enough to show it. So the line is coloured instead, by how far off the
> reference you actually were.
>
> Widest difference between these two laps: 2.06 m. Measured, and the metres are
> printed on the legend so the colour cannot flatter anybody.

### 12 · The overlay
> Open your video beside the recording, line it up once, and the telemetry is
> rendered straight into the exported file. No second program.
>
> Eleven instruments. Drag any of them anywhere. Reorder them and nothing moves
> — the order decides what covers what, never where a thing belongs.
>
> No throttle or brake bars on this one: the car in this recording does not
> report them, and an empty tube beside a working one is a lie about the
> driving.

### 13 · Two laps, one video
> Two laps side by side, paired by place rather than by clock — so the faster
> one does not simply run away.
>
> Look at the speeds. At this point on the circuit the slower lap is the faster
> car: 153 against 151 km/h, and it is still 0.20 s down.
>
> On a stopwatch that is invisible. Side by side it is obvious.

### 14 · The library
> Open it with nothing loaded and it still has something to say.
>
> Every circuit you have ever driven, drawn — with how many recordings sit on
> each and what your best there is. The one you drove most recently wears the
> accent.
>
> Clicking a circuit offers its recordings rather than opening one. You pick the
> day.

### 15 · The track has no internet
> So none of this needs any.
>
> Local mode is a real mode, not a degraded one. The puck plugs into USB, so you
> can pull a session in the paddock between runs with a laptop on the boot lid.
> Video renders on your machine. Even the typefaces are bundled, because a font
> that fetches itself is a font that is missing when it matters.
>
> No account. No upload. No signal needed.

### 16 · Twelve things
> Twelve things RDM Studio does that you would otherwise do by eye.
>
> Lap timing · continuous delta · corner report · the ideal lap · drift scoring
> · slip angle with its error bar · grip circle · moments · synced video ·
> overlay export · trust panel · twelve CAN channels from the car.
>
> Windows, macOS and Linux.
