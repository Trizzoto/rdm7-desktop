# The drive to Mallala — what the ring says (2026-08-22)

Read off the puck over USB while it sat on the bench, sweeping the whole trace
ring sector by sector: **168 105 samples** across all 1 632 sectors.
The ring holds two drives.

| | when | span | moving | distance | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Friday evening | Fri 18:34 → 19:17 | 42.6 min | 34.8 min | 38.6 km | 134.8 km/h |
| **Saturday — the drive to Mallala** | **Sat 11:20 → 12:51** | **90.8 min** | **77.2 min** | **99.5 km** | **146.1 km/h** |

Saturday runs from the house in the hills to **−34.4165, 138.5048**, which is
Mallala, arriving 12:42, then eleven minutes of paddock crawl. Twelve CAN
channels are live for all of it. This is the first recording that has real ECU
traffic across a long drive, so it is the first one that can be used to check
the recorder rather than the analysis.

The ring was **wrapped and full**, so the next drive would have overwritten
Saturday. It is saved and the puck has since been cleared (session 3, ring
empty, recording still armed). In `Documents/RDM sessions/`:

| file | what |
|---|---|
| `puck-ring-2026-08-22.jsonl.gz` | the raw sweep, 168 105 rows, both drives, channel columns as logged counts |
| `puck-ring-2026-08-22.meta.json` | `trace.info`, the channel table, the track and device info at download |
| `puck-session-2026-08-22.csv` | Saturday decoded, 115 848 rows, same columns Studio's own CSV export writes |
| `puck-session-2026-08-21.csv` | Friday decoded, 52 257 rows |

Scales in the CSVs come from the MaxxECU 1.3 preset in `ecu_presets.c`, not
from guesswork; the one column that preset does not describe is headed
*inferred*. A quiet channel is an empty cell, never a zero.

## What is proven, not just plausible

Every one of the twelve channels lands inside its physical range, first time,
with no coaxing:

| channel | measured over the drive |
|---|---|
| RPM | 1 016 – 6 611 rpm |
| Throttle | 0.0 – 99.9 % |
| Vehicle speed | 0.0 – 144.8 km/h |
| Gear | 0 – 5 |
| MAP | 15.2 – 212.1 kPa |
| Lambda | 0.62 – 2.40 |
| Ignition | 5.5 – 25.0 ° |
| Coolant | 40.7 – 95.2 °C |
| Intake air | 14.5 – 31.8 °C |
| Fuel pressure | 250.9 – 311.1 kPa |
| Battery | 13.29 – 14.44 V |

The one that settles it is **vehicle speed against the puck's own GPS speed**:
106 206 samples above 20 km/h, correlation **r = 0.9985**. Twelve channels, one
of them independently checkable, and it checks. The extraction path — bit
offset, width, endianness, the 16-bit slot, the stale sentinel — is correct on
real traffic at real rates.

Two things fall out of it for free:

- **The ECU reads 2.3 km/h slow.** Median (ECU − GPS) is −2.30 km/h, ratio
  0.974, consistent the whole drive. That is a tyre-circumference constant in
  the MaxxECU, not a fault — but it is worth knowing before anyone trusts the
  ECU's speed over the puck's.
- **`0x527` byte 6 is Lambda Target.** It is not in `signals.c`, which stops at
  byte 4 of that id. Its value tracks lambda across every boost band:

  | MAP | 0x527 b48 | actual lambda |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 – 60 kPa | 1.000 | 1.313 |
  | 60 – 100 | 0.975 | 0.961 |
  | 100 – 140 | 0.852 | 0.851 |
  | 140 – 180 | 0.797 | 0.772 |
  | 180 – 230 | 0.777 | 0.731 |

  Target flat at 1.000 on vacuum, pulled to 0.78 on full boost, with actual
  sitting just rich of it. That is a lambda target and nothing else. Worth
  adding to the MaxxECU table (scale 0.001).

## Fault 1 — a lost fix in the middle of a sector is invisible, and it makes a lap read FAST

**This is the one that matters.**

`trace_log_read()` dates every sample as `sector.base_itow_ms + slot * 40`. One
timestamp per sector is only enough while the sector is *contiguous*, and
`trace_log_sample()` guarantees that in one direction only:

```c
if (pvt->fix_type < 3 || !(pvt->flags & 0x01))
    return;                       /* <- skips the sample, leaves the page OPEN */
```

The idle path below it closes the page on purpose, precisely so a pause starts a
fresh sector with its own true time. **The lost-fix path does not.** It skips
the sample, keeps the sector open, and does not count a drop — so the reader
fabricates a flat 40 ms across the hole and the recording looks continuous.

Caught once on Saturday, at index 86679, 11:43:21, at 106 km/h:

```
  86679  817  77   526401400    40  106.6  -34.976144 138.678419    1.2 m
  86680  817  78   526401440    40  135.0  -34.972773 138.675810  443.9 m   <-- 444 m in "40 ms"
  ...      27 samples of 3-10 m steps labelled 40 ms ...
  86708  818   0   526414480 11960  105.0  -34.972703 138.676398    1.0 m   <-- sector edge, clock catches up
```

444 m of road inside one 40 ms step. The missing ~13 s does not appear where it
happened — it surfaces 28 samples later at the sector boundary, because that is
the next place a real timestamp exists.

Why it is worse than a plain hole: lap times come from interpolated crossing
instants on these timestamps. **A lap containing one of these reads about 13
seconds faster than it was driven**, with nothing anywhere disagreeing — the
trace is smooth, the cadence is 40 ms, and `gpRunGapMs` sees no gap to flag. It
is a phantom personal best generator.

The fix is small and already written elsewhere in the same file: when a sample
is skipped while `s_moving`, close the page the way the idle path does, so the
next fix opens a sector carrying its own `base_itow_ms`. Gate it on the itow
actually having jumped (more than ~1.5 sample intervals since the last recorded
one) so a single glitched fix does not burn a sector.

Second, smaller point from the same event: sample 86680 reports **135.0 km/h**
between two samples at 106. The first fix after a reacquisition passed
`fix_type == 3` while its velocity solution was still settling. `sacc_mm_s` is
already in the PVT and is already reported by `gps.status`; the sample path
never looks at it.

## Fault 2 — the writer stalls for seconds, and it is not the CAN bus

Four breaks land **exactly on a sector boundary** with the car at speed, and for
all four the elapsed clock and the road travelled agree, so the timestamps are
honest — the samples simply were not taken:

| at | duration | speed | road missed | samples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11:32:13.9 | 1.7 s | 83 km/h | 39 m | ~42 |
| 11:32:32.5 | 6.1 s | 110 km/h | 183 m | ~153 |
| 11:34:03.4 | 4.4 s | 117 km/h | 143 m | ~110 |
| 11:34:50.1 | 3.6 s | 80 km/h | 81 m | ~91 |

Plus three of ~0.1 s elsewhere. Landing on slot 0 four times out of four is the
signature of the flush path, not of the GNSS: the odds of a random outage
landing on a boundary four times running are 1 in 10⁸. This is
`s_fill_count >= rps && s_flush_pending` → `s_dropped++`, i.e. the page filled
while the previous sector was still being written. An erase is meant to be
30–45 ms. These are up to **6.1 seconds**.

`s_dropped` would have counted them, and `trace.info` reports it — but it resets
on reboot, and opening the USB port reboots the puck, so the count was gone
before it could be read. **The drop count belongs in the recording, not only in
a live status field**; as it stands Studio can never know a downloaded session
has holes in it.

What it is **not**: I expected CAN bus-off storms starving `writer_task`
(priority 3, below `can_rx` at 5, `serial_rpc` at 4 and `gnss` at 6). The data
says no. Bus-quiet density in the 60 s around each stall was 2.66 %, 3.13 %,
**0.00 %** and 2.66 % against a session average of 1.80 % — and the genuinely
bad windows (12.5 % and 11.7 % quiet, around index 120 810 and 140 310) had no
stall at all. Ruled out.

Still open. All four sit inside one 2.6-minute window (11:32–11:35) on the
fastest part of the drive and there is nothing for 77 minutes either side, which
says *event*, not *steady starvation*. Worth trying next: log
`esp_timer_get_time()` around the erase and the write separately in
`writer_task`, and count how often `s_flush_pending` is still set when a page
fills. That separates "flash was slow" from "the task never ran".

Note also that the 1.7 s one is **below** Studio's `GP_RUN_GAP_MS` of 2000, so
it would not be flagged even though 39 m of road is missing.

## Fault 3 — the puck loses the slower ECU ids, and counts none of it

Measured arrival intervals, taken from how long each channel's value holds:

| id | median | 90th percentile |
|---|---|---|
| 0x520 (RPM, MAP, lambda) | 40 ms | 120 ms |
| 0x522 (vehicle speed) | 120 ms | 920 ms |
| 0x530 (battery) | 120 ms | 720 ms |
| 0x537 (fuel pressure) | 120 ms | 720 ms |

A 90th percentile of 720 ms against a 120 ms median is not a broadcast
schedule, it is loss. It shows up as staleness: the `0x520`/`0x521`/`0x522`/
`0x527` group is stale **1.8 %** of the drive, but `0x530`/`0x536`/`0x537` are
stale **7.4 %** — about 650 separate outages, median 0.08 s, longest 4.9 s.
On the graph those are holes in the coolant, oil, fuel-pressure and battery
lanes.

Separately, **every** outage of the fast group was the whole group at once
(2 079 of 2 086 samples) — 138 whole-bus dropouts, 88 s total, longest 4.8 s.
That is the bus going away, not an id going away.

Two things to change:

- `g.rx_queue_len = 10` in `can_node.c`. The dash was on the IDF default of 5,
  which cost it a third of its frames; it now uses **64**. The puck's rx task
  does three things per frame (`trace_log_can_frame`, `rdm_bus_node_on_frame`,
  the command filter) at priority 5, and 10 slots is roughly a 20 ms burst at
  these rates.
- There is **no rx-side counter at all** — `gps.status` reports `can_tx`,
  `can_tx_drop_full` and `can_tx_drop_err` and nothing about reception. Enable
  `TWAI_ALERT_RX_QUEUE_FULL` and count it, so the next version of this question
  is answered by a number instead of by inference from hold times.

## Fault 4 — Studio's download scan steps over records it should have

Replaying `gpTraceDownload`'s hole scan against the real sector map of this
ring:

| | records | round trips |
|---|---|---|
| truth (sector-exact sweep) | 168 105 | 886 |
| Studio's `GAP_PROBE` + binary search | 167 219 | 1 290 |

It misses **886 records in 27 blocks** and takes **32 % more reads** to do it.
The cause is the binary search: it assumes the 512-wide window is
empty-then-populated, and a sparse ring is not monotonic, so a populated block
narrower than the probe step can be searched straight past.

Impact on this ring is mild — every missed record was taken at **36 km/h or
below**, mostly paddock and the first 422 samples of Saturday (the crawl out of
the driveway). But one of those blocks is the *start of the session*, and the
scan has no way to know what it skipped.

The comment in the overlay says scanning by sector "would be the tighter fix now
that the granularity is known, but the node does not report it". The node
reports everything needed to derive it: `record_bytes` is in `trace.info`, and
`(4096 − 64) / record_bytes` is exactly `recs_per_sector` — 106 here. Cheapest
correct version is both ends: have `trace.info` state `recs_per_sector`
outright, and have Studio fall back to the 4032 arithmetic when it is absent.

## Order I would take them in

1. **Fault 1** — it silently fabricates fast laps. Small change, same file, the
   pattern already exists three lines away.
2. **Fault 3's counter** — one alert and one integer, and it turns the CAN
   question from argument into measurement.
3. **Fault 4** — Studio-side, correctness *and* a third off the download time.
4. **Fault 2** — instrument first, fix second. Do not guess at this one.
5. Lambda target into the MaxxECU table; `sacc_mm_s` gate on the sample path.
